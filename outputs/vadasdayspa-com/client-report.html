Owner checklist

Five to ten minutes, ideally in one sitting — most of these are dashboard checks or quick calls.

  1. Pick one business name and retire the rest. We found three in active use — "Vada's Day Spa," "Vada's Day Spa and Salon," "Vada's Day Spa and Beauty Salon" — across your website, Google Business Profile, and a dozen directories. Gates: every citation fix, the Google Business Profile cleanup, and the AI-search identity fix.
  2. Confirm Google Business Profile category is "Day spa," not "Nail salon" or "Beauty salon" (one directory already lists you under Nail Salons). Gates: your local-pack rankings for every service you actually sell.
  3. Decide the homepage's target city: keep "Lansing" (bigger catchment, already ranking #1) or switch to "East Lansing" (matches your address, your reviews, and every competitor that outranks you on service pages). Gates: homepage title, meta description, H1, and schema.
  4. Confirm the suite number — "#300" appears on some listings, is missing on others. Whichever way, it needs to match everywhere. Gates: every NAP citation and the site's structured data.
  5. Confirm your current, active service list — in particular whether permanent makeup, hair styling, and couples massage are still offered, since a couples-massage page is a strong opportunity but shouldn't be built on a guess. Gates: the couples-massage page and the /makeup page decision.
  6. Is the nursing background okay to use in marketing? Your bio mentions you were a nurse before becoming a spa specialist — a strong trust signal for AI search and for readers, but only if you're comfortable with it in client-facing copy.
  7. Pricing publication policy — you already publish package prices; are you comfortable with individual service prices (per massage, per facial) appearing on the site too? This affects how competitive the massage and facials pages can be.

Methodology

How this audit was built — and what upgrading it unlocks

In the interest of the trust this report asks for, here is exactly what kind of audit this is.

This was a research-only pass

No paid ranking tool, no Google Search Console, no Google Analytics, and no Google Business Profile data connection were available this run. A live crawl of vadasdayspa.com was also blocked by this environment's network policy. Every finding in this report — every score, every ranking claim, every review count — comes from live Google search results, review-platform listings, and directory pages, gathered on July 15, 2026, and cited at the source. Nothing here is invented; where a number would normally sit, we marked it "pending" instead.

Connecting any one of the following converts this from a qualitative, evidence-based audit into a fully quantitative one — with real search volumes, ranking positions, traffic estimates, and Google-native review and profile data:

  1. A keyword and ranking data source — adds search volume, keyword difficulty, true position tracking, and a competitor traffic-value comparison.
  2. Google Search Console access — adds real query, click, and impression data instead of visible-in-search sampling.
  3. Google Analytics access — adds real session, source, and booking-conversion data.
  4. A Google Business Profile data connection — adds live review replies, profile insights (calls, direction requests, website clicks), and native Google review counts.
  5. Crawl access to vadasdayspa.com — adds page speed, mobile behavior, schema verification, and on-page structure checks this pass couldn't reach.
